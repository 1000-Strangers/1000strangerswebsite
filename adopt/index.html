2 weeks can change everything.

Most adoptions take about two weeks, from your first enquiry to their first night home. Step through it below.

Your adoption · tap a stop or press next
01 · Day 1
Find them
You

Browse a rescue’s animals online, or call in. Ask about personality as well as looks.

The rescue

Tells you what they know: history, habits, and who they get on with.

02 · Days 1–3
Send a short form
You

Answer a few questions about your home and your routine.

The rescue

Reads it to find the right match. They are matching, not judging.

03 · Week 1
Meet them
You

Visit and walk the dog or sit with the cat. Bring the people you live with.

The rescue

Introduces you and answers questions. If you already have a dog, they may arrange for the two to meet.

04 · Weeks 1–2
A home visit
You

A volunteer calls round for a chat and a look at where they will live.

The rescue

Checks the basics, like a secure garden or windows, and may suggest small fixes.

05 · Week 2
Bring them home
You

Sign the adoption form, pay the adoption fee (usually €120–250) and take them home.

The rescue

Hands over their vet record and microchip details. They are already vaccinated and neutered.

06 · After
You are not on your own
You

Call whenever you have a question, big or small.

The rescue

Stays on the end of the phone and helps with settling in.

Time so farDay 1

One adoption fee, usually €120–250, paid when they come home. It covers vaccines, microchip and neutering.

You might notice
—Hiding or staying in one spot
—Sleeping a lot, or eating little
—Watching everything, unsure of the rules
What helps
—A quiet corner that is theirs
—Few visitors for now
—Letting them come to you

Can't adopt or foster?
Volunteer.

Most rescues in Ireland run on volunteers. A few hours a week, or one Saturday a month, helps.

Find a rescue near youMost rescues post volunteer calls on their Facebook page. Message them and ask what they need.
Walk dogsGet kennel dogs out for exercise and time with people.
Kennels and careCleaning, feeding, and sitting with shy animals so they get used to people.
DriveBring animals to vet appointments, foster homes and new families.
FundraiseBag packs, collections, events and charity shop shifts.
Your skillsPhotography, social media, admin, repairs and building work.

Each rescue runs its own volunteering. Many ask volunteers to be 16 or over, and some ask for Garda vetting or a short induction. Contact the rescue to find out what they need.
